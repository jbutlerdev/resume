[HIRING MANAGER],

I'm applying for [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. [WHY THIS COMPANY: one or two sentences,
specific to their product or problem. Cut this paragraph entirely rather than
write something generic.]

The short version of my background: 10+ years owning platform systems across
high-scale commerce, enterprise cloud, and defense. At Shopify I owned the
Subscriptions and Pre-orders APIs that every third-party subscription app builds
on, redesigned a core data model that owned data it shouldn't and could only hold
one instance of it, and shipped the change with a live backfill of existing records.
Before that: hybrid-cloud monitoring at IBM, and mission systems for DoD customers
at General Dynamics.

The part I care more about is what happens after I ship something. A pattern
shows up across all three jobs:

- At Shopify I built an agent that routed, categorized, and drafted root-cause
  analysis for incoming support requests. The team adopted it, which let us absorb
  intake while burning a backlog of ~20 resiliency issues down to zero in 4 months.
  I also wrote code-review agent skills that kept catching bugs and design flaws
  human reviewers were missing; teammates picked them up on that track record,
  not because anyone told them to.
- At IBM I published a generalized ETL collection framework that replaced one-off
  legacy Go and Python collectors. It was adopted into the product and became the
  foundation for gNMI device collection.
- At General Dynamics I started an innersource community that grew to 100+
  reusable projects, and presented the model to 130+ engineers.

Three different companies, three different problems, same result: the thing
outlived my involvement and changed how a group of engineers worked. That's the
work I want to keep doing, and it's what I think separates a staff engineer from
a senior one.

My current focus is autonomous agent systems: orchestration, durable execution,
and the infrastructure underneath. Two things I've built in the open are Forge
(github.com/jbutlerdev/forge), a durable coding-agent platform in Rust with
PostgreSQL persistence and SSE streaming, and Mule (github.com/mule-ai/mule), a
Go workflow platform with custom tools and WebAssembly modules. Both are real
systems, and the code is there to read.

Happy to go deep on any of the above.
